# Axioms of the Essential

These are the universal laws that govern high-quality, minimalist UI design. They are independent of technology and framework.

## 1. The Law of Justification
**Every pixel must justify its existence.**
If an element (border, shadow, icon, word) does not serve a clear functional purpose or provide critical information, it is noise. Removal is the default action.

## 2. The Axiom of Typographic Dominance
**Typography is the interface.**
In the absence of decoration, typography must carry the hierarchy, tone, and structure. Use size, weight, and whitespace to guide the user, not colors or boxes.

## 3. The Principle of Material Honesty
**Use materials for what they are.**
On the web, materials are HTML elements. A button should look like an interactive element, not a physical object from the 19th century. Avoid skeuomorphism and "faking" depth with gradients and shadows.

## 4. The Law of Proximity and Void
**Whitespace is a structural element.**
Void is not "empty space"; it is a tool for grouping and separation. Generous whitespace reduces cognitive load and allows the "Essential" to breathe.

## 5. The Rule of the Silent Grid
**Structure must be felt, not seen.**
Align elements to a consistent mathematical grid. The alignment itself provides the order; you don't need lines or containers to show where things start and end.

## 6. The Paradox of Choice
**Less choice leads to more action.**
Limit options to the primary goal. If a screen has more than one primary action, it is not yet "Essential."
